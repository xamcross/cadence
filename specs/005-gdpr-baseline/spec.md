# Feature Specification: GDPR Baseline — Consent, Erasure & Audit Log

**Feature Branch**: `005-gdpr-baseline`
**Created**: 2026-06-15
**Status**: Draft
**Input**: User description: "take the next unimplemented task from the backlog. do review with sub-agents" → resolved to **F04 — GDPR Baseline: Consent, Erasure & Audit Log** (next unimplemented item in the delivery sequence after F00 scaffold, F01 authentication, F02 RBAC, and F03 workspace configuration; backlog Delivery Sequence: F00 → F01 → F02 → F03 → **F04** → P1).

## Overview

F00 stood up the platform, F01 established **who** a member is, F02 established **what** each role may do, and F03 made the *workspace* a configurable thing — including the **data-retention period** it acknowledged but deferred *enforcement* of to this feature (F03 FR-022). Up to now no **candidate** has existed in the system: the candidate is the data subject whose personal data GDPR governs, and every later feature (scheduling, messaging, ATS import) creates and acts on candidate records.

F04 is the **GDPR baseline**: the foundation that makes candidate personal data lawful to hold and process *before* any feature starts creating candidates. It is a foundation feature in the same spirit as F01/F02/F03 — it ships the data-subject record's GDPR-critical core, the privacy primitives, and the Administrator-facing surfaces that have a home today, and it defines the **binding contracts** that the candidate-creating and candidate-communicating features (F13, F22, F30, F40/F41/F42) MUST honour when they land.

F04 delivers four privacy guarantees, all keyed to a candidate by an internal, non-PII identifier:

1. **Lawful-basis (email-channel consent) tracking** — a candidate's personal data may not be used to email them until a lawful basis for email contact is recorded (timestamp + basis). F04 owns the consent record and the **contact-permission gate primitive** that the email channel (F22) calls before every dispatch.
2. **Right to erasure (the shared wipe mechanism)** — an Administrator or Recruiter can erase a candidate's personal data; a candidate can request erasure via their status page (routed to an Administrator for confirmation); and the data-retention policy from F03 drives age-based erasure. **All three converge on one shared wipe** that replaces name/email/phone with an erased marker, makes the record non-identifying, and writes an immutable audit record.
3. **Append-only per-candidate audit log (FR-18)** — every message, booking, and material change to a candidate is recorded in an append-only log keyed by the candidate's internal id, holding **non-PII identifiers only**, with no update or delete path. The audit log survives erasure (it is the accountability record). F04 ships the log, the append primitive, and the Administrator view; later features append their own event types through the same primitive.
4. **Retention enforcement (consumes F03's configured period)** — a scheduled scan flags candidate records older than the workspace retention period, the contact-permission gate denies communication to flagged records, and an Administrator confirms permanent deletion through the shared wipe.

Because the candidate-*creating* surfaces (recruiter add, ATS import, CSV import, scheduling intake) and the candidate-*communicating* surface (email) are owned by later features, F04 ships and enforces today everything that has a surface — the candidate GDPR record, the consent/erasure/audit/retention primitives, the Administrator and Recruiter endpoints, RBAC enforcement, encryption-at-rest of candidate PII, append-only auditing, and the zero-PII-in-logs guarantee — and defines the forward contracts the consuming features honour. No new infrastructure service is introduced; candidate PII reuses the F01 application-level encryption-at-rest approach.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Lawful basis is recorded before a candidate is emailed (Priority: P1)

The system holds, for each candidate, a record of the lawful basis for contacting them by email (the basis and the moment it was established). No outbound email may be sent to a candidate unless a lawful basis is on record and the candidate has neither been erased nor had their basis withdrawn. The actual email send is owned by a later feature (F22); F04 owns the recorded basis and the permission gate the email channel consults.

**Why this priority**: Sending a candidate email with no recorded lawful basis is a GDPR violation on the product's primary (and, for the MVP, only) communication channel. This gate is the precondition for every outbound communication the product will ever send, so it must exist and be correct before any sending feature is built. It is demonstrable end to end today: a seeded candidate with no basis is denied by the gate; once a basis is recorded the gate permits; after erasure or withdrawal it denies again.

**Independent Test**: Seed a candidate with no recorded basis and confirm the contact-permission gate denies email contact with a "no lawful basis" reason; record a lawful basis and confirm the gate permits; withdraw the basis (or erase the candidate) and confirm the gate denies again with the correct reason — all without any email actually being sent (F22 owns sending).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a candidate with no recorded lawful basis for email, **When** the contact-permission gate is consulted, **Then** it returns *deny* with reason "no lawful basis recorded" and no email may be sent.
2. **Given** a candidate, **When** a lawful basis for email contact is recorded (basis value + timestamp + the acting member or system actor), **Then** the basis is persisted and the gate returns *permit* on the next consultation.
3. **Given** a candidate with a recorded basis, **When** the basis is withdrawn (opt-out), **Then** the gate returns *deny* with reason "withdrawn" and the withdrawal is recorded in the candidate audit log.
4. **Given** a candidate whose personal data has been erased, **When** the contact-permission gate is consulted, **Then** it returns *deny* with reason "erased", irrespective of any prior basis.
5. **Given** the lawful-basis record, **When** the application is restarted, **Then** the recorded basis and its timestamp are read back unchanged.
6. **(F22 forward contract — not exercised by F04's test suite)** **Given** the F22 email channel, **When** it attempts any dispatch, **Then** it MUST consult this gate first and abort the send on *deny*, recording a non-PII dead-letter — F04 specifies and unit-tests the gate primitive; F22 wires it into the dispatch path.

### User Story 2 - Administrator or Recruiter erases a candidate's personal data (Priority: P1)

An Administrator or a Recruiter triggers erasure of a candidate's personal data. The system replaces the candidate's name, email, and phone with an erased marker, makes the record no longer identifiable or findable by those values, and writes an immutable audit record of the erasure. The request is acknowledged immediately (it does not block on the size of the candidate's history); completion is recorded in the audit log.

**Why this priority**: The right to erasure (GDPR Art. 17) is a non-negotiable legal obligation and the single most consequential privacy action the product performs. The shared wipe it establishes is reused by the candidate-initiated path (Story 4) and retention-driven deletion (Story 5), so it is the foundational primitive of the whole feature. It is fully demonstrable today against a seeded candidate.

**Independent Test**: As an Administrator (and again as a Recruiter), trigger erasure of a seeded candidate; confirm the response is an immediate acknowledgement; confirm the candidate's name/email/phone are replaced with the erased marker, the candidate is no longer findable by email, an immutable erasure audit record exists, and the candidate's internal id and audit trail survive; confirm a Hiring Manager, Interviewer, and Read-only user are each refused (HTTP 403).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a candidate with personal data and a signed-in Administrator, **When** the Administrator triggers erasure, **Then** the candidate's name, email, and phone are replaced with the documented erased marker, the candidate becomes non-identifying (and not findable by its former email), and an immutable audit record (actor, candidate internal id, timestamp, outcome) is written.
2. **Given** the same capability, **When** a Recruiter triggers erasure, **Then** it succeeds identically (Admin and Recruiter are the two authorized triggers).
3. **Given** an erasure request, **When** it is accepted, **Then** the API acknowledges within 2 seconds without blocking on the candidate's audit/history volume, and erasure completion is recorded in the audit log.
4. **Given** a Hiring Manager, Interviewer, or Read-only user, **When** they attempt to trigger erasure, **Then** the request is refused with HTTP 403 and nothing is erased.
5. **Given** an already-erased candidate, **When** erasure is triggered again, **Then** it is a benign no-op (no error, no second wipe, no contradictory audit) — erasure is idempotent.
6. **Given** an erased candidate, **When** the candidate audit log is read, **Then** the audit entries still exist and reference the candidate by internal id only, containing no name/email/phone (the audit survives and is itself non-PII).
7. **Given** a non-existent or already-erased candidate id, **When** erasure is triggered, **Then** the response is indistinguishable from the success/no-op path and does not reveal whether personal data ever existed (no existence oracle).

### User Story 3 - Append-only per-candidate audit log (Priority: P1)

Every material event in a candidate's lifecycle — record created, lawful basis recorded/withdrawn, message sent, booking made/changed, stage/status change, erasure requested/confirmed/completed — is recorded in an append-only audit log keyed by the candidate's internal id. An Administrator can view the full audit log for a candidate. No path exists to update or delete an audit entry. Entries contain only non-PII identifiers and event codes, never the candidate's name, email, phone, or message content.

**Why this priority**: FR-18 ("full audit log of every message, booking, and change per candidate") is a stated MVP requirement and a GDPR accountability control; the erasure and consent stories are only trustworthy if their actions are recorded immutably. The audit mechanism must exist before the features that generate audited events (messaging, scheduling) are built, so they can append through it. It is demonstrable today for the event types that exist now (create, consent, erasure, retention).

**Independent Test**: Drive the candidate through the events available today (create → record basis → request/confirm/complete erasure); confirm each produces exactly one append-only audit entry with non-PII content; confirm an Administrator can read the full ordered log for the candidate; confirm there is no API or code path that updates or deletes an audit entry; confirm a non-Administrator is refused the read.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** any audited candidate event, **When** it occurs, **Then** exactly one audit entry is appended (candidate internal id, actor id or system, event code, timestamp), containing no name/email/phone or message content.
2. **Given** a candidate with several audited events, **When** an Administrator reads the candidate's audit log, **Then** the full set is returned in chronological order.
3. **Given** the audit log, **When** any actor attempts to modify or delete an existing audit entry, **Then** no such path exists (append-only) — verified by the absence of any update/delete endpoint and an append-only contract test.
4. **Given** a non-Administrator, **When** they attempt to read a candidate's audit log, **Then** the request is refused with HTTP 403.
5. **Given** an erased candidate, **When** the audit log is read, **Then** all pre-erasure entries remain present and non-identifying, plus the erasure entries (the log is the accountability record and is never wiped).
6. **(F13/F20/F22 forward contract)** **Given** a later feature that sends a message, makes a booking, or changes a stage, **When** that event occurs, **Then** it MUST append a non-PII entry through this primitive — F04 ships the primitive and its event taxonomy; the message/booking/stage event types are appended by their owning features.

### User Story 4 - Candidate-initiated erasure request routed to an Administrator (Priority: P2)

A candidate can request erasure of their personal data (GDPR Art. 17 data-subject path). The request is recorded and routed to an Administrator, who reviews pending requests and either confirms (triggering the shared wipe) or rejects (recording the decision). The candidate-facing submission surface rides the candidate status page (owned by F30); F04 owns the erasure-request record, the routing, and the Administrator review-and-confirm workflow.

**Why this priority**: GDPR Art. 17 requires a data-subject-initiated path, not only an operator-initiated one. The Administrator confirmation workflow and the request record are fully buildable and demonstrable today; the candidate-facing trigger needs the status-page private link (F30), which does not exist yet, so that one surface is a forward contract. P2 because the operator-initiated path (Story 2) already satisfies the erasure obligation operationally; this story adds the data-subject channel.

**Independent Test**: Create an erasure request through the request-intake primitive (as F30 will); confirm it appears in the Administrator's pending-requests view; as an Administrator, confirm it and verify the shared wipe runs and the decision is audited; in a second case, reject it and verify no wipe occurs and the rejection is audited; confirm a non-Administrator cannot view or decide pending requests (HTTP 403).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a candidate, **When** an erasure request is submitted on their behalf (via the intake primitive F30 will call), **Then** a pending erasure-request record is created and an audit entry is appended.
2. **Given** one or more pending requests, **When** an Administrator views the pending-requests queue, **Then** the requests are listed using non-PII identifiers.
3. **Given** a pending request, **When** an Administrator confirms it, **Then** the shared wipe (Story 2) runs for that candidate, the request is marked resolved-confirmed, and both the decision and the erasure are audited.
4. **Given** a pending request, **When** an Administrator rejects it, **Then** no wipe occurs, the request is marked resolved-rejected, and the decision is audited (with a non-PII reason code).
5. **Given** a non-Administrator (Recruiter, Hiring Manager, Interviewer, Read-only), **When** they attempt to view or decide erasure requests, **Then** the request is refused with HTTP 403.
6. **(F30 forward contract)** **Given** the candidate status page, **When** a candidate submits an erasure request via their private-link token, **Then** it MUST call this intake primitive (token-validated, rate-limited per the F14/F30 token rules) — F04 specifies this contract; F30 wires the candidate-facing surface when the status-page token lands.

### User Story 5 - Retention-driven enforcement of the configured retention period (Priority: P2)

The workspace data-retention period (configured and acknowledged in F03) is enforced: a scheduled scan flags candidate records whose age exceeds the period, the contact-permission gate denies communication to flagged candidates, and an Administrator reviews flagged records and confirms permanent deletion through the shared wipe. The actual blocking of an outbound send happens at dispatch time (owned by F22, which consults the same gate); F04 ships the scan, the flag, the gate-deny, and the Administrator-confirmed deletion.

**Why this priority**: F03 acknowledged the retention period as a GDPR gate but deferred *enforcement* to F04 (F03 FR-022, stakeholder-confirmed). Without enforcement the acknowledged period is display-only, which the backlog (ISSUE-10) explicitly forbids. P2 because it depends on candidate records existing and reuses the Story 2 wipe; the operator and data-subject erasure paths (Stories 2 & 4) deliver the core erasure obligation first.

**Independent Test**: With a workspace retention period configured (F03), seed candidate records of varying age; run the retention scan and confirm only over-age, non-erased records are flagged; confirm the contact-permission gate denies a flagged candidate; as an Administrator, confirm deletion of a flagged record and verify the shared wipe runs and is audited; confirm the scan is idempotent and missed-fire-safe across a simulated restart.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a configured retention period and candidate records of varying age, **When** the retention scan runs, **Then** exactly the records whose age exceeds the period (and that are not already erased) are flagged for review; younger records are untouched.
2. **Given** a flagged (over-retention) candidate, **When** the contact-permission gate is consulted, **Then** it returns *deny* with reason "over retention".
3. **Given** a flagged candidate, **When** an Administrator confirms deletion, **Then** the shared wipe runs and the deletion is audited (with the retention reason); flagging alone never deletes without Administrator confirmation.
4. **Given** the retention scan, **When** it is interrupted and the application restarts, **Then** the scan resumes idempotently with no duplicate flagging and no missed window (it uses the scheduled-task checkpoint pattern).
5. **Given** the workspace retention period is shortened, **When** the next scan runs, **Then** newly-over-age records are flagged, but no record is deleted without Administrator confirmation (a policy change never auto-deletes).
6. **(F22 forward contract)** **Given** the F22 email channel, **When** it attempts to send to a flagged candidate, **Then** the shared gate denies the dispatch at send time — F04 ships the flag and the gate-deny; F22 enforces the block at dispatch.

### User Story 6 - No plaintext personal data in logs across every GDPR flow (Priority: P1)

Across every flow this feature introduces — recording a lawful basis, the contact-permission gate, all three erasure paths, the audit-log append and read, and the retention scan — no candidate personal data (name, email, phone) and no message content is ever written to application logs at any level, including DEBUG/TRACE, exception messages, stack traces, and object string representations. The continuous-integration log scan finds zero personal-data matches across these flows.

**Why this priority**: A GDPR baseline that leaks PII into logs defeats its own purpose, and the constitution (§VIII) forbids PII in logs at any level. This is a hard security gate that must hold the day the feature ships; it is the developer-facing guarantee behind backlog US-F04-5. P1 because it is a release-blocking gate, not polish.

**Independent Test**: With logging at its most verbose level, drive a candidate through record-basis → audit → erase (operator and candidate-initiated) → retention-flag → confirm-delete and the gate consultations; scan the captured logs and confirm zero occurrences of the seeded candidate's name, email, or phone (and zero message content); confirm the candidate record's string representation and any validation/exception path also emit no PII.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** any GDPR flow in this feature, **When** it logs, **Then** it logs only the candidate's internal id and non-PII event/outcome codes — never name, email, phone, or message content.
2. **Given** the full record-basis-erase-retention run at DEBUG/TRACE, **When** the CI log scan runs, **Then** it finds zero personal-data matches (the scan covers the candidate PII patterns, not only the member patterns the existing scan covered).
3. **Given** the candidate record and any erasure/validation exception, **When** their string/error representations are produced, **Then** they contain no plaintext name/email/phone.

### User Story 7 - Administrator GDPR surfaces in the frontend (Priority: P3)

A signed-in Administrator reaches a dedicated area to view a candidate's audit log and the pending erasure-request queue, and a Recruiter or Administrator can trigger erasure from the candidate context. Non-authorized roles do not see these controls and, on direct navigation, are sent to the `/not-authorized` page (per F02), not a broken screen. All GDPR UI strings use the project's localization mechanism.

**Why this priority**: The server is the security boundary (Stories 2–5); the frontend is usability and defense-in-depth. It is P3 polish required by the Definition of Done but never the sole gate. (Because the candidate-management surfaces are owned by later features, F04's frontend is a minimal Administrator/Recruiter GDPR surface keyed by candidate internal id, not a full candidate browser.)

**Independent Test**: Sign in as an Administrator and confirm the audit-log view and pending-erasure queue are reachable; as a Recruiter, confirm erasure trigger is reachable but the audit-log/queue (Admin-only) controls are not; as each unauthorized role, confirm the controls are hidden and direct navigation redirects to `/not-authorized`; confirm the underlying APIs independently refuse the unauthorized call; confirm all strings are localized.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an Administrator, **When** they open the GDPR area, **Then** they can view a candidate's audit log and the pending erasure-request queue.
2. **Given** a Recruiter, **When** they act in a candidate context, **Then** they can trigger erasure but cannot view the Admin-only audit log or request queue (controls hidden; API refuses with 403 on direct call).
3. **Given** a role not authorized for a given GDPR control, **When** they navigate directly to its route, **Then** they are redirected to `/not-authorized` (not a 404/500) and the API independently refuses the call.
4. **Given** any GDPR route guard, **When** exercised in a frontend unit test, **Then** the authorized role(s) pass and **each** unauthorized role is redirected to `/not-authorized`.
5. **Given** any GDPR UI string, **When** it renders, **Then** it uses the project's localization mechanism (no hard-coded user-facing text).

### Edge Cases

- **Email with no lawful basis**: The contact-permission gate denies; no send may occur. Recording a basis flips the gate to permit; withdrawal or erasure flips it back to deny.
- **Erasure idempotency**: Erasing an already-erased candidate is a benign no-op — no error, no second wipe, no contradictory audit. Concurrent erasure triggers on the same candidate resolve to a single wipe (race-safe), both attempts audited or the loser a benign no-op.
- **Erasure existence oracle**: Triggering erasure on a non-existent or already-erased candidate id returns a response indistinguishable from the normal path, so the API cannot be used to probe whether a person's data was ever held (mirrors F02's indistinguishable scoped not-found).
- **Audit survives erasure**: Erasure wipes the candidate's PII fields but never the audit log; audit entries reference the candidate by internal id only and remain as the accountability record. There is no path that deletes audit entries, including during erasure.
- **Erasure does not block on history size**: The erasure API acknowledges within 2 s and completes the wipe without blocking on the volume of the candidate's audit/message history; completion is recorded in the audit log.
- **Candidate-initiated request without a status page (today)**: F04 ships the request-intake primitive and the Administrator confirm/reject workflow; the candidate-facing submission (status-page token) is wired by F30. Until F30, the data-subject path is exercised through the intake primitive (as F30 will call it).
- **Basis re-recorded after withdrawal**: Withdrawing a basis flips the gate to *deny: withdrawn*; recording a basis again flips it back to *permit*. The full record → withdraw → re-record cycle is a supported, audited transition.
- **Erasure-request double / concurrent decision**: Confirming or rejecting an already-resolved request is a benign no-op/409 (no second wipe); two Administrators confirming the same pending request resolve to a single wipe (guarded transition, F02 CAS pattern).
- **Retention period shortened or lengthened**: Shortening flags newly-over-age records (nothing deleted without Administrator confirmation). Lengthening — or activity refreshing the last-activity timestamp — causes the next scan to **clear** a now-stale flag and the gate to return *permit*. A retention policy change never itself deletes data (consistent with F03 FR-022).
- **Erased then re-applied**: After erasure the candidate is non-identifying and not findable by its former email; if the same person later re-applies, a **fresh independent record** is created (no resurrection of the erased record's data or audit linkage).
- **Retention scan idempotency / missed fire**: The scan uses the scheduled-task checkpoint pattern (F00.2) so a mid-scan restart neither double-flags nor skips a window.
- **Non-identifying after erasure**: After erasure the candidate is not findable by its former email (the lookup key is removed/neutralized), so a re-import of the same person creates a fresh record rather than resurrecting erased data.
- **Audit/consent before candidate creation (forward)**: Candidate *creation* is owned by F13/F40/F41/F42; those features MUST create the record through F04's canonical-record contract so the GDPR defaults (no basis yet, not erased) and the creation audit entry are guaranteed centrally, not re-implemented per feature.
- **Non-PII audit content**: An audit entry for a "message sent" event records the event code and candidate internal id, never the message subject/body or recipient address (the message content is PII and is never audited or logged).
- **Single workspace (MVP)**: Candidate records are keyed by `workspaceId`; there is one workspace in the MVP (consistent with F01–F03).

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Candidate GDPR record & lawful basis

- **FR-001**: System MUST persist, per candidate, a record holding the GDPR-critical attributes: an internal non-PII identifier, the workspace identifier, the candidate's personal data (name, email, phone) **encrypted at rest**, a lookup key derived from the email that does not expose the email, the recorded lawful basis for email contact (basis value + timestamp + acting actor) and its withdrawal state, and the erasure state. The record MUST be read back unchanged across application restarts.
- **FR-002**: System MUST store candidate personal data (name, email, phone) **encrypted at rest** using the application's existing non-deterministic authenticated at-rest approach (the same approach used for member PII), such that a raw datastore read returns ciphertext, never plaintext; and MUST look up a candidate by email via a keyed, non-reversible derived value (never by querying the encrypted field), consistent with the F01 member-PII pattern. The email-lookup index MUST be **non-unique** (a person may legitimately be a candidate for more than one requisition; candidate-email uniqueness is not an invariant, and de-duplication is a later-feature concern — F42); this also avoids the F01 unique-partial-index null-collision footgun when erasure clears the lookup key for multiple candidates.
- **FR-003**: System MUST record a candidate's lawful basis for email contact (the basis value from a closed enumeration, the timestamp it was established, and the acting member or system actor referenced by **internal id only**) and MUST allow that basis to be **withdrawn** (opt-out, GDPR Art. 7(3)); both recording and withdrawal MUST append a candidate audit entry. System MUST expose an **operator surface** (Administrator or Recruiter) to record and to withdraw a candidate's lawful basis, so the gate (FR-004) is demonstrable end to end today; the candidate-facing self-service withdrawal surface is owned by F30 (forward contract).
- **FR-004**: System MUST expose a **contact-permission gate** that, given a candidate, returns *permit* only when a lawful basis is on record AND the basis is not withdrawn AND the candidate is not erased AND the candidate is not flagged over-retention; otherwise it MUST return *deny* with a non-PII reason code. The gate MUST **fail closed (deny)**: on any error, a missing/unknown candidate, a null/unrecognized state, or any condition it cannot evaluate, it MUST return *deny*, never *permit*. When more than one deny condition holds, the reason code MUST follow a defined precedence: **erased > over-retention > withdrawn > no-basis**. This gate is the single decision point the email channel (F22) MUST consult before every dispatch (forward contract).
- **FR-005**: System MUST provide a **canonical candidate-record creation contract** (the single seam through which candidate records are created) that establishes the GDPR defaults — not withdrawn, not erased, not retention-flagged — appends a "record created" audit entry, and accepts an **optional** initial lawful basis (so a creating feature that has a basis, e.g. recruiter-asserted legitimate interest, records it atomically; absent one, the gate stays *deny: no-basis* until a basis is recorded — fail-closed default). F04 ships **no HTTP candidate-create endpoint**: candidate *creation surfaces* (recruiter add, ATS import, CSV import, scheduling intake) are owned by F13/F40/F41/F42 and MUST create records through this contract; F04 owns the contract and the GDPR-critical subset of the record, and exercises it production-path via integration tests (not hand-built fixtures).

### Right to erasure — the shared wipe

- **FR-006**: System MUST provide a single **shared wipe mechanism** that, for a given candidate, (a) replaces the candidate's name, email, and phone with the documented erased marker (the literal constant `[ERASED]`, per the backlog acceptance criterion); (b) **overwrites the stored email-lookup key to empty/null** so no value derived from the former email remains in the document — the key being *unqueryable* is insufficient; the prior derived value MUST be destroyed so the subject cannot be re-identified by recomputing it from a known email; (c) sets the erasure state and timestamp; and (d) appends an immutable erasure audit record (actor or source referenced by internal id only, candidate internal id, timestamp, non-PII reason). The recorded lawful-basis values MAY be retained as consent/withdrawal **evidence** (Art. 7(1) accountability) provided they reference no re-identifying data (internal ids only); all directly-identifying fields (name/email/phone and the email-derived key) MUST be destroyed. All erasure paths (operator-triggered, candidate-initiated, retention-driven) MUST use this one mechanism. The erasure audit record MUST be written only by the path that actually performs the state transition (so a no-op/loser path writes no duplicate).
- **FR-006a (erasure-participant forward contract)**: Every later feature that stores any copy of candidate personal data outside the F04 candidate record (e.g. rendered message bodies, ATS payloads, dead-letter records, scheduling artifacts) MUST make that store **reachable by the shared wipe** so a single erasure de-identifies the subject everywhere. F04 owns the wipe and this contract; each such feature registers its PII store with / is invoked by the wipe when it lands. (F04 itself holds no such auxiliary store; this binds the future.)
- **FR-007**: System MUST allow an **Administrator or a Recruiter** to trigger erasure of a candidate's personal data; any other role MUST be refused with HTTP 403 and no erasure performed.
- **FR-008**: System MUST acknowledge an erasure trigger within 2 seconds without blocking on the volume of the candidate's audit or history records, and MUST record erasure completion in the candidate audit log (the wipe is performed so it does not hold the request open on large histories).
- **FR-009**: System MUST make erasure **idempotent**: triggering erasure on an already-erased candidate is a benign no-op (no error, no second wipe, no contradictory audit), and concurrent erasure triggers on the same candidate resolve to a single wipe. The response for a non-existent or already-erased candidate MUST be **indistinguishable** from the normal path, so the endpoint is not an existence oracle for whether a person's data was ever held.
- **FR-010**: System MUST preserve the candidate audit log through erasure — erasure MUST NOT delete or alter audit entries — so that the audit log remains the immutable accountability record after the candidate's PII is gone. Audit entries reference the candidate by internal id only and therefore remain non-identifying.

### Candidate-initiated erasure request

- **FR-011**: System MUST persist an **erasure-request record** representing a data-subject (candidate) request, with states pending / resolved-confirmed / resolved-rejected, keyed by the candidate's internal id, and MUST append an audit entry when a request is created. The request record MUST contain **no candidate free-text PII** — it carries the candidate internal id and a bounded non-PII reason code only; any candidate-supplied free text MUST be rejected or sanitized at intake (the F01 dead-letter sanitization precedent: do not trust the caller), so the request record does not itself re-identify an erased subject.
- **FR-012**: System MUST allow an **Administrator** to view pending erasure requests (using non-PII identifiers) and to **confirm** a request (running the shared wipe, FR-006) or **reject** it (recording a non-PII reason); both decisions MUST be audited. Request state transitions MUST be **guarded** (only *pending* → *resolved-confirmed*/*resolved-rejected*): confirming or rejecting an already-resolved request is a benign no-op/409 (no second wipe), and concurrent confirmations of the same request resolve to a single wipe (the F02 last-Admin CAS pattern). Viewing or deciding erasure requests MUST be refused for every non-Administrator role with HTTP 403.
- **FR-013**: System MUST provide an **erasure-request intake primitive** for the candidate-facing path that accepts only a candidate identifier and a bounded non-PII reason code; the candidate-facing submission surface (the status-page private-link token, with token validation and rate-limiting per the F14/F30 token rules) is owned by F30 and MUST call this primitive (forward contract). F04 owns the intake primitive and the Administrator confirm/reject workflow.

### Per-candidate audit log (append-only)

- **FR-014**: System MUST maintain an **append-only** per-candidate audit log keyed by the candidate's internal id, recording every material candidate event (record created, basis recorded/withdrawn, erasure requested/confirmed/rejected/completed, retention flagged/cleared/deleted, and — via forward contract — message sent, booking made/changed, stage/status changed). Each entry MUST contain only **closed-enumeration codes** (event code, outcome/reason code), the candidate internal id, the actor (member internal id or system), and a timestamp — **no free-text value fields** that could carry candidate-derived data, so "non-PII" is structural, not merely intended.
- **FR-015**: System MUST NOT provide any path to update or delete an audit entry (append-only), consistent with the F01/F02 audit rule; there MUST be no mutation or deletion endpoint, and a contract test MUST assert the append-only property. The candidate audit log is retained as the legitimate-interest accountability record (it survives erasure, FR-010); the MVP applies no maximum-retention ceiling to it (an accountability-justified, consciously-accepted exception to storage-limitation, recorded here rather than left silent).
- **FR-016**: System MUST allow an **Administrator** to read a candidate's audit log in a **deterministic total order** (timestamp, then a monotonic insertion sequence as tiebreaker, so entries sharing a timestamp order deterministically); a read for a candidate with no entries returns an empty list and a read for an unknown candidate id returns a response that is not an existence oracle (consistent with FR-009). The read MUST be refused for every non-Administrator role with HTTP 403.
- **FR-017**: System MUST NEVER record candidate personal data (name, email, phone) or message content in an audit entry; a "message sent" or "booking" event records the event code and candidate internal id only, never the message subject/body or recipient address.

### Retention enforcement (consumes F03's configured period)

- **FR-018**: System MUST run a **scheduled scan** that flags candidate records whose age **strictly exceeds** the workspace data-retention period configured in F03 (a record at exactly the period boundary is NOT flagged), excluding already-erased records. The **age basis is the candidate's last-activity timestamp** (`lastContactAt`, the GDPR "last processing/contact" semantics), not record creation, so the F00.1-pre-declared `{ workspaceId, lastContactAt }` index supports the scan with an erasure-state residual filter. The scan MUST use the shared scheduled-task checkpoint pattern (F00.2) so it is idempotent and missed-fire-safe.
- **FR-019**: System MUST cause the contact-permission gate (FR-004) to return *deny* with reason "over-retention" for a flagged candidate, MUST require explicit **Administrator confirmation** before a flagged record is permanently deleted through the shared wipe (FR-006), and MUST **clear a stale flag** when a subsequent scan finds the record no longer over-age (e.g. the retention period was lengthened or activity refreshed the last-activity timestamp), flipping the gate back to *permit* (assuming a basis is on record). Flagging alone MUST NOT delete data, and changing the retention period MUST NOT itself delete data.
- **FR-020**: System MUST treat the F03-configured retention period as the binding policy input (F03 FR-022 contract): F04 reads the configured period and enforces it; the *dispatch-time* blocking of an outbound send to a flagged candidate is enforced by F22 consulting the same gate (forward contract). F04 ships the scan, the flag, the gate-deny, and the Administrator-confirmed deletion.

### Access control (reuses F02)

- **FR-021**: System MUST enforce, on the **server**, the following role authorization for every F04 endpoint, consistent with the F02 deny-by-default model, such that a direct API request by an unauthorized role cannot perform the action regardless of what the frontend exposes: **erasure trigger** and **record/withdraw lawful basis** → Administrator or Recruiter; **candidate audit-log read**, **erasure-request view/confirm/reject**, and **retention review/confirm-delete** → Administrator only. Every unauthorized attempt MUST be refused with HTTP 403 and no state change.
- **FR-022**: System MUST mount every authenticated F04 endpoint under the internal (non-allow-listed) API prefix so it is subject to the F02 endpoint-inventory test and the authenticated security chain; any candidate-facing intake surface (F30-owned) rides the candidate (public, token-validated) chain and MUST NOT expose any operator capability.

### Logging & secret-safety (constitution §VIII)

- **FR-023**: System MUST NOT write candidate personal data (name, email, phone) or message content, in any encoding, to application logs at any level (including DEBUG/TRACE), nor to exception messages, stack traces, or object string representations (per the F01 dead-letter sanitization precedent); the record-basis, gate, erasure, audit, and retention flows — including their error paths — MUST pass the project's PII log scan with zero matches.
- **FR-024**: System MUST extend the continuous-integration PII log scan to cover the candidate PII patterns introduced by this feature (name/email/phone of a candidate), so the zero-PII guarantee (FR-023) is verified automatically and does not rely solely on the member-only patterns the existing scan covered.

## Key Entities *(include if feature involves data)*

- **Candidate (GDPR record)**: The data-subject record keyed by an internal non-PII id and the `workspaceId`, holding the candidate's personal data (name, email, phone) **encrypted at rest**, a keyed non-reversible email-lookup value, the recorded lawful basis for email contact (+ timestamp, actor, withdrawal state), and the erasure state (active / erased, with the erasure timestamp). F04 owns this GDPR-critical subset; later features (F13/F40/F41/F42) create the record (through F04's contract) and extend it with non-GDPR-core attributes (stage, requisition, etc.).
- **Lawful-basis / consent state**: The recorded basis for emailing the candidate (basis value, established timestamp, acting actor) and its withdrawal state. The input to the contact-permission gate.
- **Erasure request**: A data-subject-initiated request (pending / resolved-confirmed / resolved-rejected) keyed by candidate internal id, with the Administrator decision and a non-PII reason; the candidate-facing submission surface is F30-owned.
- **Candidate audit event**: An append-only, candidate-keyed, non-PII record of a material candidate event — **closed-enumeration codes only** (event code, outcome/reason code), candidate internal id, actor id or system, and timestamp (with the record's own insertion-ordered identifier as a deterministic tiebreaker); **no free-text value field**. Survives erasure; the legitimate-interest accountability record (no MVP max-retention ceiling, FR-015). Distinct from the F01/F02/F03 member-keyed auth audit log.
- **Retention flag**: The state marking a candidate as over the workspace retention period, pending Administrator-confirmed deletion; causes the gate to deny and is cleared only by deletion (the shared wipe) or by the record no longer being over-age (re-evaluated by the scan).
- **Contact-permission decision**: The gate result (*permit*, or *deny* + non-PII reason: no-basis / withdrawn / erased / over-retention) consumed by F22 before every dispatch.

## Assumptions

- **F04 is foundation, not a candidate-management UI**: F04 sits in P0 Foundation, *before* any candidate-creating feature (F13 scheduling intake, F40/F41 ATS import, F42 CSV import). It therefore introduces the candidate record's **GDPR-critical core** and the privacy primitives, not a candidate browser or candidate-creation form. Candidate *creation* is owned by the later features, which MUST create records through F04's canonical-record contract (FR-005) so the GDPR defaults and creation audit are centralized. F04 demonstrates the full lifecycle today via test seeding through that same contract. This mirrors F03's "ship everything with a surface today; bind contracts for surfaces that don't exist yet."
- **Retention enforcement binds in F04 (consumes F03; confirmed — Clarifications 2026-06-15)**: F03 acknowledged the retention period and, stakeholder-confirmed (F03 FR-022), deferred *enforcement* to F04. The stakeholder has **confirmed retention enforcement ships inside F04** (not split to a separate item): F04 owns the scan, the flag, the gate-deny, and the Administrator-confirmed deletion (US5 / FR-018–FR-020). The *dispatch-time* comms-block remains enforced by F22 consulting the shared gate (no outbound path exists in F04).
- **Email channel gate is enforced at dispatch in F22**: The backlog states the consent/erasure state is "checked by the EmailSender before every dispatch (enforced in F22)." F04 ships the consent/erasure/over-retention **state** and the **gate primitive**; F22 wires it into the dispatch path. F04 verifies the gate primitive directly; it does not send email.
- **Candidate-initiated submission surface is F30**: The data-subject erasure (and consent-withdrawal) submission rides the candidate status-page private link, owned by F30 (token TTL/rate-limit per F14/F30). F04 ships the intake primitive and the Administrator confirm/reject workflow today; F30 wires the candidate-facing trigger when the status-page token lands.
- **"Consent" = lawful-basis tracking (single channel, MVP)**: The MVP's only candidate communication channel is **email** (SMS/WhatsApp are deferred to v1.5, backlog Deferred table), so F04 tracks the lawful basis for **email** contact only. The product spec §6 phrase "lawful basis tracking" and the backlog phrase "email-channel consent" are treated as the same control; "consent" is recorded as a lawful-basis value (which may be consent or another GDPR basis) plus its withdrawal state. SMS/WhatsApp consent management is deferred with those channels.
- **Default lawful basis = fail-closed (confirmed — Clarifications 2026-06-15)**: The canonical-create contract records **no** lawful basis unless the creating feature supplies one, so the gate stays *deny: no-basis* until a basis is explicitly recorded (via the operator surface, FR-003, or supplied atomically at create by F13/F42). This is the safest GDPR default (no candidate can be emailed without a recorded basis). The stakeholder has **confirmed fail-closed**: there is **no workspace-default auto-basis** in the MVP.
- **Erasure is synchronous (reconciles the backlog "202/async" AC)**: The backlog F04 AC says erasure "returns 202 Accepted within 2 s; async completion recorded." F04 performs the wipe **synchronously** because it is O(1) — one targeted single-document update plus one audit append, with the audit log *preserved* (not deleted), so there is nothing large to cascade. The 2-second/non-blocking performance bound is met trivially; the exact success status code is a plan.md decision. If a later feature adds a genuine candidate-PII cascade (e.g. F22 message archives via FR-006a), that feature moves erasure to the constitution-compliant `TaskScheduler` and the 202/async shape, with completion in the audit log. The backlog's "async" is treated as a performance bound, not a literal threading mandate.
- **Subject-access / portability / rectification out of F04 scope (deliberate)**: F04 is the GDPR baseline for **lawful basis (Art. 7), erasure (Art. 17), and accountability auditing (Art. 5(2))** — the three controls in the backlog F04 entry. The right of **access/portability (Art. 15/20)** and **rectification (Art. 16)** are not in the backlog F04 entry and are **deferred to a later feature**; named here so the omission is a conscious, reviewable boundary rather than a silent gap (the candidate audit log already provides the data foundation a future SAR export would read).
- **EU data residency out of F04 scope (infra/Enterprise)**: §6's "EU data residency option" is a deployment/infrastructure concern (Atlas single-region, F00) and an Enterprise-tier feature, not a data-subject control F04 owns; out of scope here, recorded so the §6 omission is deliberate (mirroring how F03 documented its §5.4 "languages" omission).
- **Backup residency acknowledged (crypto-shred eventual control)**: Erasure de-identifies the live datastore; encrypted point-in-time backups retain the pre-erasure ciphertext until backup expiry. This residual is GDPR-defensible because the backup is unreadable without the workspace key and expires on the backup-retention window; key-rotation/crypto-shredding is the eventual hard control. Recorded so backup residency is not a silent erasure gap.
- **Audit log is non-PII and append-only by construction**: The per-candidate `auditLog` is keyed by the candidate internal id and records event codes only, so it both survives erasure and is itself zero-PII. "Every message, booking, and change" (FR-18) is satisfied by recording the *event* (not its content); the message/booking/stage event types are appended by their owning features (F22/F13/F51) through F04's primitive (forward contract). The audit *mechanism* and the event types that exist today are complete in F04.
- **Encryption-at-rest and audit reuse F01/F00 infrastructure**: Candidate PII reuses the existing application-level AES-256-GCM at-rest approach and keyed-hash lookup pattern from F01 (member PII); the candidate `auditLog` is a sibling of the existing member-keyed auth audit log; no new dependency, service, queue, or store is introduced (constitution §IV / gate C2). The scheduled retention scan reuses the F00.2 scheduler-checkpoint pattern; no broker.
- **Indexes are partly pre-declared (F00.1)**: F00.1 already bootstrapped `auditLog { candidateId: 1, occurredAt: -1 }` and `candidates { workspaceId: 1, lastContactAt: 1 }` at scaffold. F04 adds any further indexes it needs (e.g. a keyed-email lookup index and a retention-scan predicate index) via a new Mongock change unit; the exact set is a plan.md decision.
- **Single workspace (MVP)**: Candidate records are keyed by `workspaceId`; the MVP runs one workspace (consistent with F01–F03). Multi-workspace support later does not re-architect this feature (the key is already present).
- **HTTP semantics**: 401 = unauthenticated (F01); 403 = authenticated-but-unauthorized (F02). A non-authorized role hitting any operator GDPR endpoint receives 403. The erasure endpoint deliberately returns an indistinguishable response for non-existent/already-erased candidates (FR-009) rather than a revealing 404.
- **No retention/erasure of *member* data**: F04 governs **candidate** personal data (the data subjects). Member PII retention/erasure is out of scope (members are operators governed by F01/F02); this is a deliberate boundary.

- **C2 — no new service/queue/replica**: Satisfied. Single instance + MongoDB only; candidate PII encrypted in MongoDB; audit log and erasure-request records in MongoDB; retention scan uses the `@Scheduled` + `SchedulerCheckpoint` pattern (no broker); no object store, cache, or queue added.
- **C3 — candidate PII exposure to unauthorized roles**: Strengthened. F04's entire purpose is to minimize and govern candidate PII — encrypted at rest, never logged, Admin/Recruiter-only operator surfaces, and a lawful-basis gate before any contact. Erasure and retention enforcement actively reduce held PII.
- **C4 — fixed stack**: Satisfied. Reuses Spring Security method security, the existing PII-encryption approach, the existing audit/scheduler patterns; no new dependency.
- **C5 — script encoding**: Any new/changed `.ps1`/`.cmd`/`.bat` will be byte-scanned for non-ASCII before done.
- **C6 — multi-role sub-agent review**: Performed at spec stage — Security/GDPR, Backend/DevOps, QA, and Business Analyst perspectives (2026-06-15); findings applied (see `checklists/requirements.md`). A further review runs at each subsequent stage and at implementation task close.
- **C7 — zero tool downloads**: No build tool/runtime/CLI will be downloaded; highest already-installed versions used.
- **§VIII Security & Privacy**: Candidate PII encrypted at rest (non-deterministic, queried only via keyed hash, FR-002), never returned to or actionable by unauthorized roles (FR-021), never written to logs at any level including error paths (FR-023/FR-024), governed by a lawful-basis gate before contact (FR-004), erasable through one shared wipe across three paths (FR-006), and recorded in an append-only non-PII audit log that survives erasure (FR-010/FR-014/FR-015). The data-retention period acknowledged in F03 is enforced here (FR-018/FR-019).
